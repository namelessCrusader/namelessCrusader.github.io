WFC vs Stochastic WFC

Both generators below learn from the same small hand-drawn map: which tiles are allowed to touch, and how often. Both use the same constraint propagation. They only differ in how the next tile gets chosen:

  • Standard WFC picks a tile by how common it is in the sample overall. Neighbours only decide which tiles are allowed.
  • Stochastic WFC (mine) picks a tile by how often each option appears next to the neighbours already placed, so neighbours also change the odds.

Two settings apply to both equally: the order cells get filled in, and an optional nudge that keeps the overall mix of tiles close to the sample's.

Same-tile neighbours: share of touching tiles that are the same. Higher means larger, smoother regions; the sample's own value is the target.

Neighbour-pair match: how closely the mix of touching tile pairs matches the sample (1 minus total variation distance).

Tile mix match: the same for single tiles. It drops when one tile takes over the map.

What I see

  • SWFC makes regions; standard WFC makes speckle. Because standard WFC ignores its neighbours when sampling, any allowed tile can appear anywhere, so its same-tile score stays far below the sample's.
  • In classic order, one tile takes over. Cells next to water (or rock) have the fewest options, so both methods fill them first, and those regions keep growing. Balancing can't stop it, because the constraints themselves force the spread.
  • Row by row, SWFC shines. Each new tile is conditioned on the tiles above and to its left, which turns SWFC into a Markov chain with WFC's constraints on top. Lakes get sandy shores, and forests and mountains form patches.
  • But row by row needs balancing. On its own, the chain rarely finds its way back into water once it leaves, so lakes come out too rare. Nudging toward the sample's tile proportions fixes that for both methods, and with it SWFC gets the best pair match of any setting.
  • Neither can plan. Both only look one tile away, so neither draws a whole lake or a proper room. That would need larger patterns or a model that looks further ahead.
